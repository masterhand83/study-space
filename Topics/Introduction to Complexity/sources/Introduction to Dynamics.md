## While: short bullets, my words only

- Dynamics: how things change ==over time==
- Characterizing: "how a complex behaviour unfolds"
- Iteration of simple behaviors
- Dynamics are everywhere: Planetary, Fluid, Turbulence, Electrical, Climate, Crowd, Population, stock prices, Group, Social.
- Dynamical Systems Theory: branch of mathematics about how systems change over time.
- Dynamics of a system: how a system changes.
- The effect of its past and the cause of its future.
- Small differences in the initial conditions produce very great ones in the final phenomena. That is the introduction for **Sensitive dependence on initial conditions**
	- Butterfly effect? yes
	- Some systems might drift too much of the prediction depending of the initial condition.
	- It is the same as **Chaos**
- So there is a difference between chaos and randomness.

## After (book closed, 3 min): what do I remember?

Well, the video explains in a short way how certain authors and philosophers tried to give sense to the world. One would argue that we could make an exact prediction of the future if we know the initial conditions of our existences. But another author argued that; first, we don't know the exact initial conditions of existence we can only approximate it; second, even if we have an approximation, some systems tend to drastically change their end-state due to a very small condition change. This is equivalent to the butterfly effect and that is where we are introduced to the **sensitive dependence on initial conditions** or "chaos" in simple words: The tendency of a system to change it's end state because of an initial condition.
