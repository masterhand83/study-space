# Study method: what this vault uses and why

Every rule in this vault traces back to one of these findings. If a habit isn't here, it's not part of the system.

## What we use (strong evidence for adults)

| Method | What it looks like here | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Retrieval practice** | Flashcards, AI quizzes, "book closed" recall in the `Source` template | One of only two techniques rated *high utility* in Dunlosky et al. (2013). Large meta-analyses agree (Adesope et al., 2017). |
| **Spacing** | The Spaced Repetition plugin schedules reviews. The 10-min minimum keeps them going. | The other *high utility* technique (Dunlosky 2013). Meta-analysis by Cepeda et al. (2006). Adaptive schedulers beat fixed intervals. |
| **Interleaving** | Card reviews mix all 3 topics. `mix quiz`. The "Which service?" note for AWS. | Works best for telling similar things apart, like choosing between AWS services (Brunmair & Richter, 2019 meta-analysis). |
| **Generation / own words** | "In my own words" sections. The AI never writes your notes. | Producing an answer beats reading one (generation effect). Copied notes are a different, weaker activity. |
| **Pretesting** | "Before: guess first" in the `Source` template | Guessing before learning improves later recall, even when the guesses are wrong (Pan & Carpenter, 2023 review). |
| **Self-explanation** | "Why does it work?" in the template, plus AI `explain-back` | Moderate to strong effects (Bisra et al., 2018 meta-analysis). |
| **Drawing** | Excalidraw sketches, drawn *from memory* and then compared | Drawing what you learned beats rewriting it (the drawing effect: Wammes, Fernandes and colleagues, 2016 onward). |
| **Concrete examples** | "Concrete example" in the `Concept` template | Abstract ideas transfer better when attached to specific cases. |

## What we dropped (outdated or weak)

| Dropped | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Highlighting, rereading | Rated *low utility* (Dunlosky 2013). They create a feeling of fluency, not memory. |
| Learning styles ("I'm a visual learner") | No evidence that matching teaching to a "style" helps (Pashler et al., 2008). Drawing helps **everyone** because it forces generation, not because of a style. |
| "Handwriting beats typing" as a rule | The famous laptop study (Mueller & Oppenheimer, 2014) failed to replicate (Morehead et al., 2019; Urry et al., 2021). What matters is processing in your own words, not the medium. |
| Cramming | Works for tomorrow, fades by next week. Spacing beats it for the same hours. |
| Fixed review schedules (Leitner boxes, "review on day 1-3-7-30") | Replaced by adaptive scheduling that responds to how well you actually remember. |
| Elaborate note systems (strict Zettelkasten, deep tag trees) | **Design choice, not a study finding.** Maintaining them competes with studying, and that was a cause of losing rhythm last time. |

## ADHD layer (adults)

| Practice | In this vault |
| --- | --- |
| **External structure beats willpower** | Daily note opens automatically, `Today.md` dashboard, daily habit reminder in TickTick |
| **If-then plans** (implementation intentions: Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006 meta-analysis) | "When TickTick pings at **15:30**, I open Obsidian and do the 10-min minimum." |
| **Tiny minimum, no catch-up** | The 10-min day counts fully. Missed days are never "owed". |
| **Visible wins** | "Win of the day" line, ticked boxes in hubs, card review streaks |
| **Low switching cost** | Cards live inside notes. The same AI rules (`AGENTS.md`) apply in every app. |

> [!note] On "I think I have ADHD"
> A formal assessment is worth it if you can get one. For adults, treatment plus structure has much stronger evidence than structure alone. This vault helps either way.
